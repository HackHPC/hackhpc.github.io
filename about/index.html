About HackHPC

Hands-on collaboration, built into the research computing pipeline

HackHPC traces back to 2018 and has since grown into a recurring network of hackathons, codeathons, training institutes, and faculty development programs — run with partners across national laboratories, research computing centers, and industry.

Mission

Give students, faculty, and researchers hands-on experience with high-performance computing, AI, and science gateways through short, intensive, mentored events — lowering the barrier to entry into research computing careers.

How it works

Each event pairs participants with mentors from partner institutions to tackle a real research-computing challenge over a few days to a few weeks, culminating in team presentations, posters, or working prototypes.

Who's involved

Events are organized in partnership with organizations like ADMI, TACC, ORNL, and the Science Gateways community (SGX3/SGCI) — see the full list on the Partners page.

History

From one hackathon to a recurring program

HackHPC began in 2018 as a pair of hackathons — HackHPC@PEARC18 that July in Pittsburgh, and Cloud HPC Hack @ SC18 that November in Dallas — built on a simple premise: put students on a team, pair them with a mentor from a national lab or research computing center, and give them a few intense days to build something real with high-performance computing. The format held up, and it multiplied. What started as one-off hackathons at partner conferences grew into a recurring, multi-track program: annual hackathons at the ADMI Symposium on Computing at Minority Institutions since 2022, a faculty-focused counterpart (FacultyHack@Gateways) built with the Science Gateways Community Institute, an "HPC in the City" hackathon series that ran alongside the Supercomputing (SC) conference for several years, and codeathons and training institutes run with the SGX3 Center of Excellence.

The program has long been organized by Je'aime Powell, Linda Bailey Hayden, Amy Cannon, Boyd Wilson, Alexander Nolte, and Charlie Dey, and its approach has been documented in its own right — the "HackHPC Model" was formalized in a peer-reviewed paper at Gateways 2022, with follow-on research on FacultyHack's format and on hackathon research more broadly published through 2024 (see Research).
